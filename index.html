Macro picture: The EU dairy complex enters October in a cautiously balanced state. After an exceptional first half of 2026 — when EU milk deliveries consistently outpaced prior-year levels by 2.5–5.1% — the pace of expansion cooled markedly in August 2026 to just +0.8% YoY. This deceleration reflects both a demanding prior-year base and increasingly adverse weather conditions across key producing regions.

Price divergence: The widest market story this month is the split between protein and fat markets. SMP prices have surged +6.5% over the past four weeks, driven by procurement tenders and institutional demand, while butter has retreated −4.0% as seasonal consumer demand softens. WMP remains firm amid export interest.

Supply signals: ZMB forecasts EU-27 full-year 2026 milk deliveries at +1.8% above 2025, revised down from earlier estimates, as the autumn correction deepens. Some member states — including the Netherlands (−1.7% in September) — have already crossed below prior-year baselines.

Key Market Themes — October 2026

SMP Recovery

EU SMP at 3.37 €/kg — strongest upward move in the complex, supported by tender activity and recovering global demand.

Butter Correction

Block butter demand remains cautious. Packaged butter demand stays robust via retailer promotions. Frozen/fresh price spread widening.

Germany Outperforms

Germany Jan–Aug +6.0% YoY — the single largest volume contributor to EU supply growth with +1.3 Mio. t additional milk delivered.

Global Supply Normalising

World export country milk supply growth slowed to +1.1% in August from +3.1% YTD average — a significant deceleration entering Q4.

SWP Firming

Sweet Whey Powder food-grade lower limit raised — now scarcely available below 2.00 €/kg in Germany amid good export demand.

Energy Moderating

Brent easing to 126.6 USD/bbl from September high of ~131 USD/bbl provides modest relief on production costs heading into winter.

全体的な市場状況：EUの酪農市場は10月に入り、慎重な均衡状態を維持しています。2026年前半は前年比+2.5〜5.1%という力強い集荷成長が続きましたが、8月には+0.8%まで急減速しました。この減速は、前年の高いベース効果と、主要産地での不順な気象条件が重なった結果です。

Panorama macro: O complexo lácteo da UE entra em outubro num estado de equilíbrio cauteloso. Após um primeiro semestre de 2026 excecional — com entregas de leite sistematicamente acima dos níveis do ano anterior em 2,5–5,1% — o ritmo de expansão arrefeceu marcadamente em agosto para apenas +0,8% em termos homólogos.

Divergência de preços: O maior tema de mercado este mês é a divisão entre os mercados de proteína e de gordura. Os preços do SMP subiram +6,5% nas últimas quatro semanas, impulsionados por concursos de aquisição e procura institucional, enquanto a manteiga recuou −4,0% com a redução sazonal da procura dos consumidores.

Sinais de oferta: O ZMB prevê entregas de leite na UE-27 em 2026 em +1,8% acima de 2025, com revisão em baixa face às estimativas anteriores, à medida que a correção de outono se aprofunda.

EU-27 Milk Supply — January to August 2026

EU-27 collective: Total milk deliveries in the EU-27 reached approximately 103.6 million tonnes in the first eight months of 2026, representing a +3.1% increase compared to the same period in 2025 — an additional 3.2 million tonnes of milk. However, this headline figure masks a sharp late-season deceleration: while January posted +5.1% growth, August came in at just +0.8%, the lowest reading of the year.

Structural normalisation: ZMB notes that this deceleration is partly attributable to the difficult prior-year base — EU deliveries had unusually risen during August and subsequent months of 2025, contrary to typical seasonal patterns. This base effect will remain a headwind through Q4 2026. Adverse weather — heat and drought in France, Austria, Poland, and parts of Germany — also weighed on August output.

Forward look: ZMB forecasts full-year 2026 EU milk deliveries at +1.8% versus 2025. The Netherlands already reported a −1.7% decline in September, and further member states are expected to slip below prior-year levels in the coming months. Feed cost pressures from the 2026 harvest — poor in quality and quantity — add structural headwinds into 2027.

Content quality: Average EU fat content stood at 4.12% and protein at 3.45% through August — both marginally higher than 2025 YTD averages, though August itself saw lower fat and protein versus the prior-year month. Butter/SMP price convergence reflects improving protein relative to fat values.

Global Milk Supply Context

Export country supply growth: The combined milk supply of the world's key dairy export countries grew by an average of +3.1% in the first eight months of 2026 — the highest level in recent years. However, August growth slowed to just +1.1%, the weakest monthly reading of the year, signalling a global supply normalisation heading into Q4.

Oceania: New Zealand led with +4.2% cumulative growth through September 2026. Australia recorded +3.8% Jan–Aug. Both markets showed slowdown in their most recent months, with New Zealand September at only +1.0% — the weakest growth since the season began, ahead of potential El Niño weather impacts.

Americas: North America grew +2.4% cumulative, led by the USA (+2.6%). South America expanded +3.3%, with Argentina (+4.1%), Uruguay (+9.2%), and Chile (+4.7%) outperforming. Brazil grew +2.2% in H1 2026. Canada remained modest at +0.4%.

Non-EU Europe: Milk volumes in non-EU European countries declined slightly (−0.3% cumulative). Russia contracted −1.0%, while the UK remained broadly stable YoY, and Switzerland saw growth from the start of the year subsequently turn negative. Belarus grew modestly +0.5%.

EU-27 生乳供給 — 2026年1〜8月

EU-27全体：EU-27の2026年1〜8月の生乳集荷量は約1億360万トン（前年同期比+3.1%、+320万t）に達しました。ただし、この見出し数字は顕著な季節後半の減速を隠しています。1月は+5.1%の成長を記録しましたが、8月はわずか+0.8%（年間最低水準）まで落ち込みました。

構造的な正常化：ZMBによると、この減速は部分的に前年高ベース効果によるものです。2025年8月以降、EUの集荷量が例年に反して上昇するという異例の動きを示していたため、この高ベース効果が2026年Q4を通じた逆風となります。また、フランス、オーストリア、ポーランド、ドイツの一部での熱波と乾燥も8月の産出量に影響しました。

見通し：ZMBは2026年EU通年乳量を前年比+1.8%と予測（下方修正）。オランダは9月にすでに前年比−1.7%を記録しており、今後数ヶ月で前年水準を下回る加盟国がさらに増える見通しです。

乳成分品質：8月の平均乳脂率は4.12%、乳タンパク率は3.45%（いずれも前年同期比でわずかに高水準）。ただし8月単月の数値は前年同月を下回りました。

Oferta de Leite UE-27 — Janeiro a Agosto de 2026

Coletivo UE-27: As entregas totais de leite na UE-27 atingiram aproximadamente 103,6 milhões de toneladas nos primeiros oito meses de 2026, um aumento de +3,1% face ao mesmo período de 2025 — 3,2 milhões de toneladas adicionais. Normalização estrutural: O ZMB aponta que esta desaceleração é parcialmente atribuível ao efeito de base — as entregas da UE em agosto e meses seguintes de 2025 tinham aumentado de forma incomum, contrariando os padrões sazonais habituais. Condições meteorológicas adversas — calor e seca em França, Áustria, Polónia e partes da Alemanha — também pesaram na produção de agosto.

Perspetiva: O ZMB prevê entregas anuais de leite na UE em 2026 em +1,8% face a 2025. Os Países Baixos já reportaram −1,7% em setembro, e espera-se que mais estados-membros fiquem abaixo dos níveis do ano anterior nos próximos meses.

Market Commentary by Product
Butter

EU butter (block) at 4.01 €/kg represents a meaningful correction of −4.0% from the previous four-week average, continuing the trend from September's elevated levels. The key dynamic is the bifurcation between packaged and block formats: packaged butter is moving very well on retailer promotions, while block demand from industrial buyers remains hesitant and wait-and-see in nature.

Inventories of frozen butter continue to trade at a discount to fresh product, with the price differential widening. As some school holiday periods end across German states, consumer demand may pick up modestly in the near term. However, structural softness in block demand suggests no imminent reversal of the broader downtrend.

SMP (Skim Milk Powder)

SMP food grade is the clear outperformer this month, gaining +6.5% over the prior four-week period to reach 3.37 €/kg. This recovery follows several consecutive weeks of significant firming, driven by procurement tenders and exhibition-related demand. The ZMB Süddeutsche Börse report (KW44) notes that after this dynamic phase, the market has calmed slightly — the most recent fixing reflected only a modest upward adjustment.

SMP feed grade prices remained unchanged, reflecting lower institutional appetite. The convergence of butter and SMP prices — after wide divergence in recent years — continues, signalling an improving relative value for protein-based usage pathways.

WMP (Whole Milk Powder)

WMP at 3.87 €/kg shows a +1.6% weekly improvement, maintaining its position as a relatively firm product. Market conditions are described as mixed: orders from Germany tend to trade toward the upper end of the price range, while product from other European origins gravitates to the lower end. Export business remains supportive.

The slight divergence between EU weekly price (+1.6% in EUR) and the world quotation (−1.0% in USD) reflects both exchange rate effects and differing reference methodologies, with the stronger EUR price suggesting firm EU domestic and regional demand.

Cheddar & Hard Cheese

Cheddar world quotations softened −3.2% since mid-October (as at 11.10.2026), with EU world quotations at approximately 3,575 USD/T, equating to around 3.25 €/kg at prevailing exchange rates. However, EU weekly reference data showed only a marginal +0.2% weekly change, suggesting the spot market has not yet fully reflected the world quotation movement.

The Süddeutsche Börse KW44 report highlights strong demand for Emmentaler and Schnittkäse (semi-hard cheese) across all channels including retail, food industry, food service, and export. Cheese production has declined seasonally and stocks are low and relatively young — these supply constraints are providing underlying price support domestically in Germany.

Sweet Whey Powder (SWP)

Food-grade SWP below 2.00 €/kg now largely unavailable in Germany. The Süddeutsche Börse KW44 report explicitly notes that food-grade sweet whey powder in Germany is "scarcely available below 2,000 €/tonne" and accordingly has raised the lower boundary of the price range. Medium-term further price increases appear possible. Export business remains strong. The feed-grade SWP market has calmed after a weaker phase.

Market Commentary — Feed Markets

EU feed costs continue to exert upward pressure on dairy farm profitability, registering a +3.5% year-on-year increase as measured by the DG AGRI composite feed index. While this marks a deceleration from the more severe feed cost surges seen in 2022–2023, the persistent positive growth rate means that feed remains a structural headwind for EU dairy producers.

The primary components of EU dairy farm feed costs include maize silage, compound feeds containing soy meal, and cereal grains. In 2026, cereal grain markets have benefited from broadly adequate global harvests, providing some offset. However, the global soy complex has remained tight due to elevated protein demand from aquaculture and monogastric livestock sectors, keeping compound feed costs above historical norms.

For German dairy farmers specifically, the strong milk price environment (EU milk equivalent at 42.8 €/100 kg; +2.3% YoY) is largely sufficient to offset these feed cost increases, supporting herd maintenance and investment decisions — as evidenced by the exceptional +6.0% growth in German milk deliveries during January–August 2026.

Looking at the broader EU landscape, the relationship between feed input costs and milk output prices — the so-called "feed-to-milk ratio" — remains broadly positive for EU producers. Countries with more significant structural feed cost challenges, particularly in Eastern and Northern Europe where some member states show declining milk deliveries, may face more acute margin pressure.

The feed market outlook for late Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 will be influenced by the outcome of Southern Hemisphere grain harvests (Argentina, Brazil), European winter crop planting intentions, and continued protein feed availability. The ZMB MarktSpiegel notes an overall EU milk production forecast of +1.8% for 2026 — a figure consistent with a farm profitability environment that, despite feed cost headwinds, remains broadly constructive.

Energy Markets & Implications for Dairy Processing

Brent crude oil was quoted at 126.6 USD/barrel as at 11 October 2026 — a level well above the 2020–2022 historical average range. Elevated energy costs affect the dairy supply chain at multiple points: farm-level heating, milking equipment, and refrigeration; transport and logistics of fresh milk and dairy products; and critically, the energy-intensive processing operations of spray drying (for SMP, WMP, and SWP production), evaporation, pasteurisation, and cold chain management.

For EU dairy processors, energy typically represents 15–25% of total processing costs for powder products. At current Brent levels, energy costs for spray-drying operations remain elevated relative to pre-2021 benchmarks. This structural cost floor provides indirect support for powder product prices — processors require higher output prices to maintain adequate margins.

The elevated oil price environment also has secondary effects through increased packaging material costs (most dairy packaging contains petroleum-derived polymers) and transport costs, both of which ripple through into retail price levels across the EU.

In the context of the EU Green Deal and the dairy sector's decarbonisation agenda, some larger EU processors have been investing in energy efficiency improvements, biogas capture from whey permeate streams, and renewable energy procurement. These investments, while reducing direct energy cost exposure over time, require upfront capital and may not fully offset current energy price levels in the near term.

The net effect of the current feed and energy cost environment is a modest but persistent margin squeeze for EU dairy producers and processors, partially offset by the firming milk and commodity prices seen in Q3–Q4 2026.

The EUR/USD exchange rate at approximately 1.10 positions the euro at a broadly neutral level relative to its medium-term trading range. At an EUR/USD of ~1.10, EU dairy exports remain cost-competitive on world markets, supporting continued export volumes. The ZMB MarktSpiegel notes that SWP export business remains strong, and EU SMP export demand is evidenced by the recent price firming.

Currency Impact on Input Costs

For EU dairy producers and processors, key commodity inputs that are priced in USD include crude oil (affecting energy costs), soy and protein feed components, and global freight. At EUR/USD ~1.10, these USD-denominated costs translate into EUR at rates that are neither exceptionally favourable nor exceptionally burdensome — a broadly neutral input cost environment from a currency perspective.

The elevated Brent oil price of 126.6 USD/bbl translates to approximately 115 €/bbl at current EUR/USD rates — still a structurally high cost level for EU dairy processors dependent on energy for drying operations.

EUR and Major Trading Partners

The EUR also moves against key dairy export market currencies: the Chinese Yuan (CNY), Japanese Yen (JPY), Saudi Riyal (SAR), and British Pound (GBP). DG AGRI data for the period reflects an overall context where EU dairy export volumes have been broadly supported by competitive EUR pricing in emerging market destinations — particularly Asia and the Middle East — which absorb significant volumes of EU SMP and WMP.

The UK remains an important destination for EU dairy products (particularly cheese and butter), and the EUR/GBP rate continues to be a relevant factor for German and Irish dairy exporters supplying the British market.

  • Ireland, Estonia drag: Notable milk supply contractions (Ireland −2.5% YTD; Estonia −11.1%) partially offset EU growth.
Analytical Commentary by Theme
1. Supply — Seasonal Transition

EU milk production is entering the seasonal low-supply phase typical of October–March. After a strong first eight months of 2026 (EU-27: +3.1% vs. 2025), the August data showing deceleration to +0.8% YoY foreshadows a period of tighter supply increments. The ZMB full-year forecast of +1.8% for EU-27 in 2026 implies that the H2 growth rate will be notably lower than H1 — consistent with the historical seasonal pattern of autumn/winter milk yield reduction.

Globally, the supply picture is more mixed. New Zealand is through September at +4.2% YoY — a supportive global supply contributor — while Ireland's ongoing contraction (−8.4% in August alone) removes a key EU exporter from the global growth contribution. Net effect: global supply growth is decelerating from its H1 2026 peak, which is supportive for commodity prices into year-end.

2. SMP Outlook

SMP food grade is the month's standout performer (+6.5% over four weeks to 3.37 €/kg). The dynamic phase appears to have largely played out, with the most recent Süddeutsche Börse fixing reflecting only a modest further adjustment. The question for Q4 is whether the accumulated price increase is durable or whether it will retrace once near-term tender demand is satisfied.

Fundamentally, the case for continued SMP price support rests on: (1) tightening seasonal EU milk supply reducing casein and whey diversion; (2) sustained export demand, particularly from Asian institutional buyers; and (3) the narrowing of the butter/SMP price spread, which removes incentives to over-process toward butter at the expense of powder.

3. Butter & Fat Outlook

Butter block at 4.01 €/kg (−4.0% vs. prior four weeks) reflects industrial buyer caution in a market where frozen inventories are at a discount to fresh and industrial demand is hesitant. This is expected to remain the tone through the immediate weeks of Q4, with a potential seasonal uplift as Christmas-driven retail demand accelerates packaged formats.

The butterfat balance will be closely watched: with cheese production declining (DG AGRI shows EU cheese output −2.8% to −3.8% YTD), the co-production of butterfat from cheese whey is also reduced, limiting cream availability. This structural factor has historically supported butter prices in winter. A recovery in block prices toward the packaged butter level remains a plausible scenario for late Q4 2026.

4. SWP & Whey Complex

The structural tightness in food-grade SWP (sub-2.00 €/kg now unavailable in Germany) reflects two concurrent dynamics: (1) strong and sustained demand from the sports nutrition, functional food, and animal health sectors; and (2) the redirection of cheese production, which co-produces sweet whey, toward more domestic consumption versus export. Despite high German milk volumes, SWP supply has not kept pace with demand growth — a dynamic that appears likely to persist through Q4 2026.

5. Geopolitical & Macro Context

The elevated Brent oil price at 126.6 USD/bbl reflects ongoing global supply management by OPEC+ and geopolitical risk premiums embedded in crude markets. For EU dairy, the primary transmission mechanism is through energy costs for drying and processing, as well as logistics and packaging. These cost pressures provide an indirect floor under commodity prices.

The EUR at approximately 1.10 vs. USD provides a broadly neutral competitive position for EU dairy exporters. Should the EUR strengthen materially above 1.15, EU export competitiveness in USD-denominated markets would face headwinds — a scenario worth monitoring given potential shifts in US and ECB monetary policy through early 2027.

EU生乳生産は10〜3月に特有の季節的低供給フェーズに移行しています。ZMBの2026年EU-27通年予測である+1.8%は、H2成長率がH1を大幅に下回ることを示唆しており、秋冬の生乳収量低下という歴史的な季節パターンと一致しています。グローバルには、ニュージーランドが9月時点で+4.2%と支援的な一方、アイルランドの急激な収縮（8月単月−8.4%）が世界的な供給成長への寄与を削減。純効果：世界供給成長は2026年H1のピークから減速しており、年末に向けてコモディティ価格を支持しています。

SMP アウトルック

食品グレードSMPは4週間で+6.5%と今月最大のパフォーマー（3.37 €/kg）。動的局面は概ね終わり、最近の取引所の価格決定はわずかな上方修正を反映するにとどまります。第4四半期の焦点は、近期の入札需要が満たされた後、累積価格上昇が持続的かどうかです。継続的なSMP価格支持の根拠は：(1)EU生乳の季節的ひっ迫；(2)アジアからの継続的な輸出需要；(3)バター/SMP価格スプレッドの縮小。

バター & 脂肪アウトルック

ブロックバター（4.01 €/kg；直近4週比−4.0%）は産業用バイヤーの慎重姿勢を反映。ただし、チーズ生産の減少（EU-27で−2.8%〜−3.8%）はバターファットの副産物も減少させ、クリーム可用性を制限。この構造的要因が歴史的に冬季のバター価格を支持してきており、2026年第4四半期後半のブロック価格回復は合理的なシナリオです。

Temas Chave para o 4.º Trimestre de 2026
  • Desaceleração do crescimento do pool de leite: O crescimento anual da UE-27 em agosto abrandou para +0,8% face a +3,1% no acumulado. Desaceleração sazonal esperada durante o inverno.
  • Recuperação do preço do SMP: Forte procura institucional e atividade de concursos impulsionaram uma firmeza significativa (+6,5% nas últimas quatro semanas). Estabilização pós-fase dinâmica em curso.
  • Divergência da manteiga: Manteiga em bloco sob pressão de compradores industriais vs. procura de retalho em embalagem muito forte. Monitorizar a normalização do spread até ao final do ano.
  • Escassez de SWP: SWP grau alimentar abaixo de 2,00 €/kg estruturalmente indisponível. Suporta progressão de preços adicional a médio prazo.
  • Domínio do fornecimento alemão: Crescimento de +6,0% no acumulado da Alemanha continua a ditar o ritmo. Nenhum sinal de reversão a curto prazo.

Oferta — Transição Sazonal

A produção de leite da UE está a entrar na fase sazonal de baixa oferta típica de outubro a março. A previsão anual do ZMB de +1,8% para a UE-27 em 2026 implica que a taxa de crescimento do 2.º semestre será notavelmente inferior à do 1.º — consistente com o padrão sazonal histórico de redução do rendimento leiteiro no outono/inverno. A nível global, a Nova Zelândia contribui positivamente (+4,2% até setembro), enquanto a contração contínua da Irlanda (−8,4% só em agosto) retira um exportador-chave da contribuição global para o crescimento.

Perspetivas do SMP

O SMP grau alimentar é o produto em destaque deste mês (+6,5% nas últimas quatro semanas para 3,37 €/kg). A fase dinâmica parece ter-se dissipado em grande medida. O caso para um suporte continuado de preços do SMP assenta em: (1) aperto sazonal do leite da UE; (2) procura de exportação sustentada, particularmente de compradores institucionais asiáticos; (3) estreitamento do spread de preços manteiga/SMP.

Perspetivas da Manteiga

A manteiga em bloco a 4,01 €/kg (−4,0% vs. quatro semanas anteriores) reflete a cautela dos compradores industriais. Com a produção de queijo em declínio (−2,8% a −3.8%), a coprodução de gordura de manteiga do soro de queijo também é reduzida, limitando a disponibilidade de nata. Este fator estrutural tem historicamente suportado os preços da manteiga no inverno, tornando uma recuperação dos preços do bloco para o final do 4.º trimestre de 2026 um cenário plausível.

Contexto Geopolítico & Macro

O Brent elevado a 126,6 USD/barril reflete a gestão de oferta da OPEP+ e prémios de risco geopolítico. O EUR/USD de ~1,10 proporciona uma posição competitiva amplamente neutra para os exportadores de lacticínios da UE. Caso o EUR se valorize materialmente acima de 1,15, a competitividade das exportações da UE em mercados denominados em USD seria afetada — um cenário a monitorizar dado potenciais mudanças na política monetária dos EUA e do BCE até início de 2027.
